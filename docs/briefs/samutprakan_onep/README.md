## 1. No ONEP outline
No declared old-town boundary was found. Pak Nam's Rama II town is the strip of the east bank round the city pillar and the
provincial hall (~13.588–13.602 N, 100.595–100.602 E), read from imagery; not a legal boundary.

## 2. What the map says
- **The river is wide (~0.8–1 km) and the town sits on its east bank.** The chedi is on the west bank 1.4 km WNW of the
  pillar, ~200 m inland: the island it was built on has joined the west bank (a creek west of the temple and a mangrove
  island mid-river remain on the imagery).
- **Phi Suea Samut fort** is 0.4 km south of the chedi on the same (west) bank; **Phra Chulachomklao fort** is 6.5 km SSW at
  Laem Fa Pha, on the west side of the river mouth. The mouth is ~5.7 km south of the pillar; the coast runs WSW from it
  (fish ponds and mudflats) and ESE to **Bang Pu** (10 km SE), all mudflat and mangrove.
- **The old Paknam railway** came in from the north along the east bank (today's ถนนทางรถไฟสายเก่าปากน้ำ, then Sukhumvit)
  to the market by the river (the OSM monument "รถไฟสายแรกของไทย").
- **Far places** (off a ~13 km tile): Suvarnabhumi 19 km NE (bearing 59), drawn at the tile's north-east corner; the
  Bhumibol twin bridges 9–11 km NW (bearing 319–325), drawn where the river enters the tile at its north-west.
- Everything else is at true scale: the river, Pak Nam, the chedi, both forts, the mouth, the coast and Bang Pu.
