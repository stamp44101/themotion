## Why Yasothon (owner picked it, 10 Nov 2026)

- **A symbol the province owns, in its own motto:** "เมืองบั้งไฟโก้ แตงโมหวาน หมอนขวานผ้าขิด แหล่งผลิตข้าวหอมมะลิ".
  The bang fai (บั้งไฟ) is the first word of it; the khit axe pillow (หมอนขวานผ้าขิด) is the third. Residents build
  their own rockets for Bun Bang Fai every May, so the symbol is made by local hands, not a national monument
  (§19-§20: shares come from something each viewer names as theirs).
- **A cover half-made by hand at frame 0 that changes shape** (§18-§20): a giant bamboo rocket on its trestles, half of it
  already bound in rope; the binding walks down the rest; the tail goes on; it swings up onto its launch tower.
- **A big, close event at 3-9 s, the same object:** the rocket launched from the tower over the town, a column of smoke,
  the crowd below. The cover and the first event are one object in local hands, as Uttaradit's sword was.
- **A 0-to-100 spine (to be confirmed by research):** Ban Sing Tha on the Chi → the town raised and named (2357) → the
  old trading street of Sing Tha → under Ubon, then a province of its own (1 Mar 2515) → jasmine rice and the festival
  grown big → today's town and the May festival with the giant rocket back over it.

## Covers (style frames, 10 Nov; `yasothon_styleframes.jpg`, stills at 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 s)

All three put the hero at legend scale on the whole forest-and-paddy tile with the Chi down its west side, camera 290-340
out at 30-36 degrees, as Uttaradit's v2 frames did (owner, 8 Nov: the cover shows the whole land tile).

- **A1 (cloud's recommendation): a giant bang fai bound by hand on the land.** A bamboo body ~42 units long on two
  trestles, a red cloth nose. At frame 0 the front half is already wrapped in rope rings; a coil of rope walks down the
  rest, each ring dropping on spinning with a puff of fibre, all bound by ~2.2 s; the long bamboo tail is lashed to the
  rear, and from ~2.6 s the rocket swings up onto its bamboo launch tower (ฮ้านบั้งไฟ). The change is of **shape**
  (bare tube → bound rocket with a tail → upright), and it hands straight to the 3-9 s launch. Sound: rope creaking,
  the knock of bamboo, a drum (กลองยาว) under the binding.
- **A2: the khit axe pillow (หมอนขวานผ้าขิด) stuffed one section at a time.** Ten khit sections (red, black, cream,
  gold) in a row; the front half already plump triangles at frame 0, the rest flat cloth; a bundle of kapok walks down
  the row and each section rises into a triangle with a white puff; the finished pillow lifts and turns. A resident's
  object from the motto, gentle; but it does not hand on to a big 3-9 s event, and from this distance it is small.
- **B: Phaya Khankhak, the toad king of the rain legend, a giant on the land** (~24 units), his throat swelling as he
  croaks; at ~1.4 s a bang fai shoots up from its tower beside him with a column of smoke. Moving and surprising, but
  nothing is half-made at frame 0 (§19 says that loses); better as the legend in the payoff or the sub line.

What is placeholder in the frames: the rocket is a plain bamboo tube with rope rings (no naga head, no ลายกนก; the
decorated bang fai eh is a procession float and could come in the payoff); there is no person (the coil binds alone, as
the hammer swung alone in Uttaradit's frames); the tower is four leaning poles; the Chi's course is drawn by eye until
[local]'s map; the counter shows 2243 because the frames borrow Korat's story; the toad is made of spheres.

**Owner picked A1 (10 Nov): the giant bang fai bound by hand and swung onto its tower.** The launch is the 3-9 s
event; the pillow and Phaya Khankhak can come back in the payoff or a sub line. Research to confirm: what a traditional
Yasothon bang fai and its launch tower look like (material, binding, tail, size).
