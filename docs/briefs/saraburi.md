## Why Saraburi next

- **A link to the last clip.** The Ratchaburi resident said the Thai Yuan live in two provinces,
  Saraburi and Ratchaburi, both moved down from Chiang Saen. Saraburi is the other half of that
  journey (Sao Hai, the Thai Yuan villages on the Pa Sak); a sub line can name the link.
- **A legend with an animal in it.** The Buddha's Footprint (พระพุทธบาท) found in the reign of
  King Songtham by a hunter (พรานบุญ) following a wounded deer that drank from the print and was
  healed: the strongest hook candidate so far (big, moving, the city's own story).
- **A town that moved.** The seat was at Sao Hai and later moved to Pak Phriao on the Pa Sak, the
  town of today (which years: research).
- **0 to 100:** a small Ayutthaya-era river town, the northeastern railway, the cement works in the
  limestone hills (Siam Cement at Tha Luang / Kaeng Khoi), the Friendship Highway to Korat, and an
  industrial town at the gate to the Isan plateau.

## First dates checked on the web (1 Nov 2026)

- **Founding:** "สันนิษฐานว่าตั้งขึ้นประมาณ พ.ศ. 2092" in the reign of Maha Chakkraphat
  (กศน.อำเภอเมืองสระบุรี; สำนักงานธนารักษ์พื้นที่สระบุรี). The province's own page
  (saraburi.go.th, ประวัติความเป็นมา) says only that the town existed before **2112** (named in the
  chronicle under Mahinthrathirat) and that no document gives its founding: **2092 is a
  supposition**; a caption may say "ราว 2092" at most, or use 2112.
- **The seat moved from Sao Hai to Pak Phriao** (library.tru.ac.th, สระบุรี ประวัติ-สภาพทั่วไป):
  the year is for the research.

## Geography (Gemini 4, to be pinned by [local])

- Pak Phriao (today's town, station 14.5283, 100.9108) on the Pa Sak's south/left bank; the river
  70–100 m wide in town, coming from the NE and bending west to Sao Hai.
- Sao Hai old town (Wat Chan Thaburi / Bueng Nong Ngong) 8–10 km WNW; Ton Tan Thai Yuan village
  ~4 km WNW; Sao Hai market 6 km WNW. **Near enough for one tile** along the river.
- Far place 1: **Wat Phra Phutthabat**, 24.8 km NNW (bearing ~328°), on Khao Suwan Banphot.
- Far place 2: **Kaeng Khoi / Thap Kwang cement hills**, 11–24 km NE (~55°), with the Pa Sak rail
  bridge (2510) and the Friendship Highway going that way.
- Mondop: seven tiers, ~11–13 m square base, ~35–37 m to the finial (old text 18 วา 2 ศอก); the
  print 1.52 × 0.53 m; three naga stairs.
